Arguably the best wild green on the continent, growing as a "weed" in every garden bed, vacant lot, and sidewalk edge — abundant right now. If you learn one urban green, learn this one.

## Harvest
- Pick the tender top 10–15 cm and young leaves; strip leaves from tougher lower stems.
- It regrows after cutting, so a good patch keeps producing all season.
- Later in the season it sets abundant seeds — edible, a wild relative of quinoa, worth collecting if you're keen.

## Prep
- Cook exactly like spinach: sauté with garlic and oil, wilt into soups, eggs, dal, or pasta. It holds texture better than spinach.
- Young leaves are fine raw in small amounts, but cooking is the default (reduces oxalates, tastes better).
