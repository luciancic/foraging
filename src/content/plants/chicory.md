A cheerful roadside weed that's fully edible root-to-flower — and the classic wild coffee substitute. Blooming its sky-blue flowers right now across sunny urban edges.

## Harvest
- **Leaves:** best from the basal rosette in early spring and again in cool fall — young leaves are pleasantly bitter, older summer leaves punishingly so.
- **Roots:** dig in fall from established plants; a thick taproot, like a pale carrot.
- **Flowers:** pick fresh in the morning for a garnish.

## Prep
- **Greens:** treat like dandelion or radicchio — braise, sauté, or blanch-and-refresh to tame the bitterness; toss young leaves into salad in small amounts.
- **Roasted-root "coffee":** scrub, chop, roast the root until dark and brittle, grind, and brew like coffee — caffeine-free, deep and roasty. The traditional New Orleans coffee cut.
- **Flowers:** scatter over salads as a garnish.
