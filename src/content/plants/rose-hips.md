**What a rose hip is:** the fruit of a rose. Once the flower's petals fall, the base swells into a little red pod — that's the hip. It's a "berry" only loosely; botanically it's a fleshy pod packed with seeds. The thin outer flesh is the edible part, and it's one of the richest wild sources of **vitamin C** there is (famously used for syrup during WWII when citrus was scarce).

## Harvest
- **Not yet** — the ones you photographed are still green. They colour to orange-red through September.
- Best picked **after the first frost**, when they soften and sweeten (or pick firm and freeze overnight to fake the frost). They hang on the shrub well into winter, so there's a long window.
- Bigger hips (dog rose, rugosa) are much less fiddly than tiny ones.

## Prep — mind the seeds
The catch: inside each hip is a cluster of seeds wrapped in **fine irritant hairs** you don't want to swallow. Two ways around it:
- **Whole-cook-and-strain:** simmer whole hips soft, then push through a sieve or jelly bag — the smooth pulp/juice comes through, hairs and seeds stay behind. This is the easy route for **syrup, jelly, or purée**.
- **Deseed fresh:** top-and-tail, halve, scrape out the seeds and hairs, use the shells for tea or drying.

## Uses
- **Rose-hip syrup** — the classic, spooned over yogurt/desserts or in drinks; a vitamin-C tonic.
- **Tea** — dried deseeded shells, tart and floral (often blended with hibiscus).
- **Jelly/jam** — tart, high pectin.
