Rowan is a classic hedgerow and street tree — the orange berry clusters are unmistakable in late summer. Edible, but firmly in the **cook-don't-graze** camp: raw they're harshly bitter and a little irritating, but cooked (ideally after a frost) they make one of the great wild preserves.

## Harvest
- Cut whole clusters once the berries are fully orange-red and slightly soft, **August into fall**. They hold on the tree for months, so there's no rush.
- Best flavour comes **after the first frost** — or cheat it by freezing the picked berries overnight, which breaks down the bitterness the same way.
- Strip the berries off the stalks before cooking (the stalks are bitter).

## Prep
- **Rowan jelly** is the classic: a tart, faintly bitter amber preserve, traditionally served with game, lamb, and rich cheeses. Rowan is high in pectin, so it sets well — or blend with crabapple for extra set and a softer flavour.
- Also good cooked into a sharp sauce or added to apple dishes for a bittersweet edge.
- High in vitamin C.

## Notes
- Never eat more than a nibble raw — bitter and stomach-upsetting.
- _(Log the first-frost date each year — that's your cue that the berries have sweetened.)_
