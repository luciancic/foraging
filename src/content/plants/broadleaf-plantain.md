The most reliable "I know exactly what this is" plant in any lawn or path edge. Best known as a first-aid herb, but the young leaves are also edible.

## Harvest
- Pick young, tender inner leaves (older ones get stringy and tough).
- The green seed spikes can be stripped and eaten too, or dried — the seeds are a psyllium relative (fibre).

## Uses
- **Drawing poultice:** chew or crush a leaf and press onto insect bites, stings, and minor scrapes — a classic field remedy for the itch/sting. Genuinely useful on a foraging walk.
- **Food:** young leaves cooked like a green (blanch to soften), or shredded small into salad.
