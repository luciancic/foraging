A row of young apple trees lines the bike path (on the map) — planted or feral, either way left unpicked. Full-size apples, not crabapples, so a handful is a real snack. Fruit is on and windfalls are already dropping to the grass below.

## Harvest
- Pick when fully coloured and they part from the branch with an easy twist. Taste one first — seedling apples run anywhere from sweet to sharp-tart.
- Grab the good ones off the tree; use windfalls quickly (they bruise) and cook rather than eat those raw.
- They hold and sweeten on the tree into fall — apples that get a frost turn noticeably sweeter.

## Prep
- **Raw** if the tree's a sweet one — otherwise they shine cooked: sauce, apple butter, pie, or a tart-sweet crumble.
- Slightly under-ripe or sharp apples are high in pectin and perfect stretched into **jelly** or added to a low-pectin fruit to help it set.
- Halve and core; no need to peel for sauce (run it through a mill after cooking).
